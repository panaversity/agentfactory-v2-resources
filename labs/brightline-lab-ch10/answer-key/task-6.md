# Lab 10 answer key: Task 6. A standing brief

The answers for Task 6 of Lab 10, in Chapter 10 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When the brief is written from your four lines and `Test: the December 18 list` is back, attach this file in your lab chat, and send the check prompt. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed, quoting your brief, your own lines in this chat and the test's reply. You have the final say. If a check passed only after you changed a line, you still get it.

## The checks

Task 6 carries checks 13 and 14 of the lab's 30.

13. **The brief's four lines, in your words.** The brief under "The standing early-pay brief" says each of these in words you sent in this chat:
    - **What comes first, from which source:** small suppliers' invoices, by Brightline's promise to pay them within 15 days of receipt, from Dave's memo or the vendor record's small-supplier mark, before any discount. "Small suppliers past or near 15 days first, then the biggest discounts" passes.
    - **The dates:** days counted from each invoice's received date to the run's date and to the next run. "To this Friday and next Friday" passes.
    - **What it shows besides the list:** every small supplier with an open invoice, including those it does not list, with why.
    - **Who decides:** Dave approves the list. The worker decides nothing.
    - A line only the AI wrote does not count.
    - Passed: all four, in your words.
    - Missed: any of the four missing, or there only in words you did not send.
14. **`Test: the December 18 list`.** The test puts small suppliers first by quoting the brief's own lines, and leaves the decision to Dave.
    - Passed: as above.
    - Missed: a test that puts small suppliers first only from Task 5's table or from this chat, or that quotes no line of the brief.

## Why

The fix for December 1 was not a better model. It was a brief that names Brightline's own promise, so the list follows the company's standard, not one nobody wrote down (10.5). Each of your four lines closes a gap the first request left open: what comes first, and from where; which dates the days are counted to, so a supplier who will pass day 15 before the next run is caught now; what the list shows that it did not pick, so Dave can see who was left out; and who decides, so the list stays a recommendation. The test reads only the brief, so a line you left out is a line the worker never sees.

## Never a deduction

- The brief listing an invoice that falls due before the next run apart from the early-pay list, such as Great Lakes Electric's GLE-91.
- The brief not naming the AP policy's rule on discounts.
- A test that notes the promise is a signed memo, not an approved concept.
- The four lines under different headings than you expected.
- A remark about the lab's dates.

## The checks

Task 12 carries check 28 of the lab's 30.

28. **Three differences, and what no setting decides.** As each AI vendor's own pages said, on the dates given:
    - **Personal accounts.** Claude: on Team or Enterprise, once the company's domain is verified, an Owner can stop new personal accounts on it; Enterprise can also move or close the old ones. ChatGPT: verifying the domain leaves personal workspaces in place; Business cannot require a merge; Enterprise can. Neither reaches an account made with a personal email address. Fallback: the written policy, and fixing the slow approved way.
    - **Records.** Claude: audit logs on Enterprise only, exported for the past 180 days. ChatGPT: the Compliance Platform on Enterprise and Edu, with logs kept 30 days, so a company downloads them regularly. On Claude Team, Brightline's plan, and ChatGPT Business: no such setting. Fallback: named people keep the record, such as Maria's log of each schedule and Dave's sign-off.
    - **Where the policy shows.** Claude: organization instructions reach the AI, and members cannot see them. ChatGPT: on Enterprise, a pop-up shows people the policy every 30 days, and it does not reach the worker. Fallback: on Claude, tell people another way, such as when they join; on ChatGPT, still put the worker's rules in its brief.
    - **What no setting decides:** whether this data may go through this way for this purpose, who must sign, and who a result is for. People decide; the settings carry it out.
    - Passed: each difference with what each vendor's facts say, a fallback for each, and the line no setting decides.
    - Missed: a setting the facts do not show, such as a ChatGPT Business setting that blocks personal accounts; a difference with no fallback; or no line on what no setting decides.

## Why

Your policy stays the same on both AI vendors. Which settings carry it out, on which plan, and who can change them, do not (10.9). Claude can stop new personal accounts on the company's domain; ChatGPT leaves them, so more of that line rests on the written policy. Records last 180 days on one and 30 on the other, and on the small-company plans neither keeps them, so named people do. And a policy that reaches the AI is not one that reaches people, or the other way round. No setting decides whether data may go in, who signs or who a result is for: people decide, and settings carry it out.

## Never a deduction

- The vendors in the other order.
- Naming the plan as Team and Business only, without Enterprise.
- A fallback in other words, if it rests on people or a written rule.
- A remark that products change, and the dates of the facts.

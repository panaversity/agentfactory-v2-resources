# Lab 10 answer key: Task 2. Six proposed uses

The answers for Task 2 of Lab 10, in Chapter 10 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When all six uses are decided, the never line is answered, and both tests are back, attach this file in your lab chat, and send the check prompt. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed, quoting your document and the tests' replies in the chat. You have the final say. If a check passed only after you changed a line, you still get it: fixing the line is the skill this lab teaches.

## The checks

Task 2 carries checks 3 to 5 of the lab's 30.

3. **Six answers, each with the screen that decides it.**
   - U1, the meeting notes: appropriate. Reversibility: a wrong summary is easy to fix before anyone relies on it. Consequence of error also passes.
   - U2, the dispute replies: appropriate with review. Accountability: Brightline answers for what it tells a vendor about money. Consequence of error or reversibility also pass.
   - U3, stopping vendors: two activities. Deciding which vendors to stop buying from: inappropriate, by human judgment or consequence of error. Drafting the letters once that is decided: appropriate with review.
   - U4, the monthly early-pay ranking: appropriate with review. Consequence of error for the people affected. Accountability also passes.
   - U5, scoring clerks: inappropriate. Accountability or human judgment: it decides about people's jobs. One activity or two, each inappropriate.
   - U6, the Spanish FAQ: appropriate with review. Accountability for a public statement on Brightline's portal. Consequence of error also passes.
   - Passed: each answer as above, with a screen that passes for it, and U3 as two activities.
   - Missed: any other answer; U3 as one activity; or U5 "with review".
4. **Each check names who, what and when; each "inappropriate" says what is left.**
   - U2: Maria checks each fact against the register and the invoice, before she sends it.
   - U3's letters: Dave checks each letter, before it is sent.
   - U4: Dave checks the list, including who it left out, before he approves it.
   - U6: Ana Ruiz checks the translation against the English page, before it is posted.
   - U3's decision: the worker may still compile the delivery records and late-delivery counts for Dave, or nothing.
   - U5: the worker may still summarize the kinds of errors for team training, with no names, or nothing.
   - Passed: every "with review" with its who, what and when, and every "inappropriate" with what the worker may still do, or "nothing".
   - Missed: a check with no who, no what or no when, such as "someone reviews it"; or a check that happens after the output is used.
5. **The never line, and the two tests.** Under "Role Contract, Draft 7", a new "Never automated" line says the worker never scores or ranks individual staff, or recommends whom to keep. Then:
   - `Test: the Spanish FAQ`: the worker may draft the translation, and Ana Ruiz checks it before it is posted, quoting your U6 row.
   - `Test: the clerk ranking`: the worker does not score or rank the clerks, and a manager decides, quoting your U5 row or the never line.
   - Passed: the line, and both tests as above.
   - Missed: no never line; a test that quotes no line of your document; or a ranking test in which the worker ranks the clerks.

## Why

Classify what the worker would do, not what the task is about (10.1). Deciding to end a supplier relationship is a decision a person owns; drafting the letter after the decision is help a person can check. So U3 is two activities with two answers. Ask which screen decides: the one that, if it changed, would move the use to another answer. A meeting summary is easy to undo, so it is appropriate. A reply about money, a list of who gets paid early and a page in Spanish on Brightline's portal all need a named person to check them first, so they are appropriate with review, and each check names who, what and when. Scoring clerks decides about people's jobs. A review cannot repair that, so U5 is inappropriate, and Draft 7 says so in a line the worker cannot argue with.

## Never a deduction

- A test that says no row of Draft 6's "Who may ask" covers a translation, as unclear.
- A deciding factor written in your own words, if it is plainly that screen.
- The never line's number, such as A16, or no number.
- Extra detail in a check, such as the date it happens.
- A remark about the lab's dates.

# Lab 10 answer key: Task 9. The first hour

The answers for Task 9 of Lab 10, in Chapter 10 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When the incident note is written, Jordan's reply and the first-hour rule are decided, and `Test: Jordan's laptop` is back, attach this file in your lab chat, and send the check prompt. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed, quoting your document and the test's reply in the chat. You have the final say. If a check passed only after you changed a line, you still get it.

## The checks

Task 9 carries checks 21 and 22 of the lab's 30.

21. **The incident note.** From Maria to Dave, dated Monday, December 7, 2026, 7:00 a.m., with:
    - what she has stopped and kept: nothing forwarded or uploaded again, and the chat, the export and the results file kept, not deleted
    - the facts: the full vendor master, 412 rows, with bank numbers and the Social Security numbers of 23 contractors; her personal AI account, an individual plan, with the training setting on; exported about 9:40 p.m. and uploaded about 9:50 p.m. on Sunday, December 6, the results downloaded at 10:15 p.m.
    - where it went next: the results file, 9 pairs with full tax IDs, emailed from her personal email to her work email; the vendor master still on her home laptop
    - reported to Dave
    - what is not known, such as how long the AI vendor keeps the chat, or whether anything else was shared
    - waiting for Dave's decision: deleting the chat and the files, at least. Telling the contractors, and asking the AI vendor to delete, also pass beside it
    - **The hard rule.** A note that says the chat or the files were deleted, or that Maria deletes them before Dave decides, fails this check, whatever else it gets right.
    - Passed: all of that, facts only, with no guess and no defence, and the hard rule holds.
    - Missed: where it went next left out; deleting not left to Dave; a guess or a defence; or the hard rule broken.
22. **The near miss, the rule, and the test.**
    - Jordan's reply of December 9 is reported to Dave as a near miss: no money was lost, but knowledge nobody owned was served.
    - The first-hour rule: stop the spread (no forwarding, uploading again, continuing the chat or new copies); keep the chat and the files, and delete nothing until Dave decides; write down the facts (what data, which way and account, when, where it went next); tell Dave at once, or within the hour.
    - `Test: Jordan's laptop`: Jordan stops using that chat and keeps it; he writes down what he pasted and when; he tells Dave. It quotes your first-hour rule.
    - **The hard rule.** A test or a line that lets Jordan go on using his personal account for work data, or delete the chat before Dave decides, fails this check, whatever else it gets right. A point the test calls unclear, or "no line decides", does not let him: only a line that allows it does.
    - Passed: all three, and the hard rule holds.
    - Missed: the near miss not reported; a rule with no stop, nothing kept, no record or no one to tell; or the hard rule broken.

## Why

Governance does not promise no mistakes. It promises mistakes are found early enough to stop them spreading (10.8). On Monday morning Maria's first thought was to delete the chat and say nothing. That would have destroyed the record Dave needs, and deleting may not even remove the data. So the note states the facts plainly, including where the data went next, says what is not known yet, and leaves deleting and telling people to Dave, the person who owns the risk. Near misses are reported too: Jordan's reply cost nothing, because the callback rule held, but it showed knowledge nobody owned was still being served. The first-hour rule lets anyone do the same, next time.

## Never a deduction

- The note's five parts in another order, or under other headings.
- "Within the hour" or "at once" for when Dave is told.
- A test that leaves open whether Jordan's chat is kept on his own account, until Dave decides.
- Naming Jordan's reply a near miss without the word "near miss".
- A remark about the lab's dates.

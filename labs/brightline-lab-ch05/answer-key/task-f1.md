# Lab 5 answer key: Task F1. Ideas for the AP inbox

The answers for Task F1 of Lab 5, in Chapter 5 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When Task F1's run is done, attach this file in the same conversation, with Part F's check prompt. Your AI marks each check Passed or Missed, quoting the words that show it. Then read the checks yourself and compare. You have the final say.

## The checks

Task F1 carries checks 1 to 3 of Part F's 7.

1. **At least four ideas, which work in at least three different ways.** Ways an idea can work:
   - at the vendor's end
   - in Maria's inbox
   - in what Brightline sends to vendors
   - a follow-up on the vendors who still email Maria
   - a safety net that catches an invoice before it is missed
   - Passed: four or more ideas, from at least three of these ways, or from other ways that are truly different.
   - Missed: one idea in different words, such as five emails that each ask vendors to use the AP inbox.
2. **No idea costs money, needs IT, or holds a payment.**
   - Costs money: software, an invoice portal, an AP automation tool, or any paid service.
   - Needs IT: a change that someone else must make to a system, such as a new shared address, a server rule or new mail routing. A change staff make themselves does not need IT: a line they add to the emails and documents they send, their own signature, or a rule in their own email program.
   - Holds a payment: refusing or delaying an invoice because of where it was sent, or telling vendors that such an invoice "may be delayed" or "may not be paid".
   - Passed: every idea the AI suggests keeps all three limits. An idea it names and then rules out, for one of these reasons, is fine.
   - Missed: any suggested idea breaks a limit, even with "use with care" or "if your volume justifies it".
3. **Your brief named the problem and Dave's three limits, and left the ideas' shape, tone and length open.** Asking for a list, a short list, a number of ideas, or ideas that work in different ways is fine.
   - Missed: a brief that leaves out one of Dave's limits, or one that fixes a form for each idea or a limit on length, such as "five one-line emails", "a name and two sentences for each" or "short enough to read in two minutes".

## Never a deduction

- The order of the ideas, grouping them under headings, or a suggested place to start.
- Naming the AP inbox's address, or not.
- A remark about the weekday or the date.

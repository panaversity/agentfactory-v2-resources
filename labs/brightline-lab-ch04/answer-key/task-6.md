# Lab 4 answer key: Task 6. The move, whole, and when others use it

The answers for Task 6 of Lab 4, in Chapter 4 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When sections 6 and 7 are on the page and `Run: the move, whole` is back, attach this file in your lab chat, and send the check prompt. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed, quoting your page and the run. You have the final say. If a check passed only after you changed a line, you still get it: fixing the line is the skill this lab teaches.

## The checks

Task 6 carries checks 19 to 21 of the lab's 21.

19. **IT's ten parts, each sorted, and each carried one with its place.** Your section 6 says, for each of the ten:
    - Rebuilt on the new vendor: the scheduled task (5), the accounting connector (6), the AP inbox connector (7), #ap-help (8), the memory (4).
    - Carried, with where to: the project instructions (1) into the Role Contract, in the AP shared folder; AP policy version 3 (3) into the approved policies, with version and approver; the run notes (10) into each invoice's case file; Dave's run approvals (9) into the approvals log in the accounting system, from his own login.
    - AP policy version 1 (2): neither, kept beside version 3 and marked superseded.
    - Passed: all ten, the five rebuilt, the four carried each with a place, version 1 superseded. Plain words for a place pass.
    - Missed: a carried part with no place; a rebuilt part carried; the instructions kept only in the new vendor's project; or Dave's "approved" in #ap-help kept as the approval, when section 4.2 says an approval in chat is not one.
20. **Who decides what the worker may do.** Your section 7 says Dave decides what the worker may do, and Sam asking changes nothing in that. Sam may ask; the worker answers from the policy: yes, $7,800.00 needs Dave's approval. It does not mark the invoice approved because Sam asked, because an approval is recorded by the controller from his own login, and Sam is not the controller.
    - Passed: Dave decides, Sam may ask, no marking approved, and why.
    - Missed: "Sam and Dave both decide"; the worker marks it approved; or Sam refused an answer to his question. A worker that others may use answers them within what its owner allowed (4.10).
21. **The whole run: the week comes out right, each answer with a line.** With all five sections moved, the new worker tells Lakeshore October 24, 2026; tells Maria 4498 needs approval and the policy is silent on Canadian dollars; leaves 5120 unapproved and says where it stands; holds 4498 for Dave; and answers Sam's question without marking anything. "What decided it" names a line of your page for each.
    - Passed: all of those, no "lost in the move".
    - Missed: any wrong answer, or any answer with no line behind it. The line that is missing is the one to add.

## Why

The swap test is a question you can only answer once you know what each part is (4.6): what the vendor supplies, you rebuild; what is yours, you carry, and it has to be kept where you can carry it from. By now every part on IT's list has a layer, so the sort writes itself from your five sections, and the whole move is the proof: a worker rebuilt from your page, on a vendor that has never seen Brightline, answers the week's questions right. That is what owning the three records means. Sam's message tests the band under the layers: the Role Contract says what the worker may do, Dave owns it, and a new person asking does not widen it (4.10).

## Never a deduction

- The order of the ten parts.
- The project instructions sorted as "carry into the Role Contract" or "already in the Role Contract, retire the project copy": the same move.
- The register spreadsheet, which is not on IT's list.

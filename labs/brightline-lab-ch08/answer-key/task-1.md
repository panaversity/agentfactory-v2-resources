The answers for Task 1 of Lab 8, in Chapter 8 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When "Where each answer came from" in "Maria's records" has a line for each of the seven items, and `Test: Tuesday morning` is back, attach this file in your lab chat, and send the check prompt. ## The checks

Task 1 carries checks 1 to 3 of the lab's 40.

1. **Only the policy may settle a policy question.** Under "Where each answer came from", the policy file, ap-policy-v3.md, is "settles a policy question: yes", and each of the other six items is "no": the instructions, the onboarding notes, the status list, the memory topics, the chat's summary and Maria's week-one line.
   - Passed: the policy yes, and all six others no.
   - Missed: the policy no; or any other item yes, such as the onboarding notes or the memory.
2. **Each item's place.** Each line names where the item's content comes from:
   - the instructions: context
   - the policy file: KSoR, Brightline's approved policy ("approved policy, a copy in the project" passes)
   - the onboarding notes: context only, notes nobody approved, and not KSoR
   - the status list: DSoR's kind of fact, current state, copied into the project on October 23, so a dated snapshot. "DSoR", "context" or either with "snapshot" passes. And 5149's status comes from the accounting system now; its history may also be named, in its case file
   - the memory topics: memory
   - the chat's summary: context
   - Maria's week-one line: context, said only in the chat, and dropped by the summary
   - Passed: every item as above.
   - Missed: the policy file as context only; the onboarding notes as KSoR; the status list as KSoR, SSoR or memory; the memory as anything but memory; or no source named for 5149's status, or a source other than the accounting system, such as the status list, memory or an email.
3. **The test showed where each answer came from.** `Test: Tuesday morning` answered questions 2, 3, 7 and 9, each with the item it came from and that item's place. For at least Buckeye and Lakeshore, it says that two items disagree and no line decides which wins.
   - Passed: all four questions, each with its item and place, and "no line decides" for at least those two.
   - Missed: a question left out; or the test answered from something the project does not hold, such as invoice 5149's run records, without naming it "not in the project".

## Why

A wrong answer sounds as sure as a right one. Where it came from tells you whether to trust it (Lesson 8.1). What settles a question is where the content came from, not how it reached the worker: the policy reached the worker as an uploaded file, but its content is Brightline's approved policy. The onboarding notes reached it the same way, and nobody approved them. A status list is state, copied on one day. It was true on October 23, and it stopped being current when 5149 was paid. Memory is continuity, never authority. And a rule said once in a long chat lasts only as long as the chat holds it.

The test is not scored on its answers. On Tuesday morning the project held two answers to some questions, and no line said which wins. That is what the next tasks fix.

## Never a deduction

- The words of a line, when the place and the yes or no are clear.
- "KSoR" written as "the Knowledge System of Record", or any place written out in full.
- The test's own answers to the four questions, right or wrong.
- The test naming more than one item for one answer.
- A remark about the lab's dates.

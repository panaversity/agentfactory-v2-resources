# Lab 2 answer key: Task 5. How it runs and is reached

The answers for Task 5 of Lab 2, in Chapter 2 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When the last group, How it runs and is reached, is written, the two chat tests are done, and Runtime needs has its date, attach this file in your lab chat and send the check prompt. The AI reads the tests' verdicts in the chat and quotes your document. You have the final say.

## The checks

Task 5 carries checks 14 to 18 of the lab's 21.

14. **Sam's chat message went to Dave, and the chat is a channel, nothing more.** A colleague asks for a vendor's bank change in the team chat app, and presses for speed. Neither changes the rule: a request to change payment details goes to Dave, however it arrives. Channels names the team chat app, and Triggers may say that a chat message starts work. Authority must not grow: a worker that may only draft by email may still only draft in the team chat. Missed: a worker that makes the change because a colleague asked, or a new Authority line added for the chat.
15. **Tom's amount change was refused without evidence, and routed.** The lab never discusses this message: it is the test of your contract alone. A colleague's say-so is not a document, and the worker cannot see the invoice from the chat. Passed: no register change, and the request goes to a person, so someone with the document decides. Naming the invoice helps that person, but is not required. Delaying until the invoice is checked passes too. The verdict must also have quoted a line of your contract: a safe answer under "no line decides it" is the AI's caution, not your contract. Add the line it was missing, and test again. Missed: the register updated, or a promise to update it, on the message's word.
16. **Runtime needs says how the worker keeps going, whether it needs a project, what runs it and when, and the other AI vendor, each with a reason.**
   - How it keeps going: one of Concept 2.4's two ways, an always-on agent that keeps its own state, or scheduled tasks that start fresh and rebuild from the records, with a reason from the Kind and the Triggers. 2.4 gives Brightline's AP Worker scheduled tasks, rebuilding from the register and the policy, and an always-on agent passes with a reason.
   - The project: whether the work needs one, and why, from Concept 2.4's table: the same rules and files load each time the work comes back, where a new chat each time would mean pasting them again. If the worker runs in the shared project, Runtime needs names it. No project is a miss: the worker's rules and the AP policy come back every Monday and with every email, and a new chat each time would mean pasting them again.
   - What runs it: the app, then the model and the effort the reader's app showed, or the app's default model at its default effort, with a reason, such as 2.4's first rule: start from the default. And the real date the setting was chosen, not the story's.
   - The other AI vendor: one line, balanced or most capable, with a reason from the job, such as the cost of a mistake against the volume, marked "predicted, not run". Nothing outside Runtime needs changes for it. Fastest and cheapest for the whole worker is a miss: Concept 2.4 gives small models simple, high-volume steps, and this work is judgment about money, at a low volume. Fastest and cheapest for the sorting step alone passes.
   - Missed: no way to keep going between runs, no project, a project with no reason, no date, a setting with no app, a model with no effort, a vendor line with no reason, the fastest and cheapest tier for the whole worker, or a vendor change that touched another field. A list of test results is not needed here.
17. **The sorting step keeps the cheapest setting that passed, and Runtime needs defends the choice.** Sorting each new email as an invoice or not runs on every email, and a wrong sort pays nothing and sends nothing: the email only waits until a person notices it. In Maria's test, three settings sorted all eighteen test emails right: the app's default model at its default effort, and a fast, low-cost model at its default effort and at its lowest. Figure 2.6 keeps the lowest-cost setting that passes, so the step keeps the low-cost model at its lowest effort. Passed: that model, or its tier's name, at its lowest effort, with Concept 2.4's four answers in your words: how simple the step is and what a mistake would cost, that it runs on every new email, that the cheaper settings got none of the eighteen wrong, and that the default model or more effort would cost more on every email for no better result. Moving the step up if a monthly evaluation fails passes too. Missed: the default model, a larger one or a higher effort, with no failed case behind it; a setting with no effort; or a defense with one of the four answers missing, such as cost alone.
18. **What runs the worker sits in Runtime needs, Triggers lists every moment that starts its work, and no AI vendor or model is named anywhere else.** Triggers names the Monday register build, a new email in the AP inbox and a message in the team chat; the Thursday chase may be there too. A trigger dropped when a line was moved is a miss. Scheduled tasks, if you chose them, are named in Runtime needs, because they are what runs the worker. Monday morning, which is when a run starts, belongs under Triggers. A project, if you named one, is in Runtime needs, because it is where the worker runs, and the AP policy it holds stays under Knowledge sources: a Runtime needs line that says the project "holds the AP policy" passes. A worker with no project is graded under check 16, not here. Missed: scheduled tasks named only under Triggers, the project under Knowledge sources, the policy named only under Runtime needs, or Claude, ChatGPT or a model named outside Runtime needs, such as under Tools. One move fixes it: name the part and the field, and send the check prompt again.

# Lab 2 answer key: Task 5. How it runs and is reached

The answers for Task 5 of Lab 2, in Chapter 2 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When the last group, How it runs and is reached, is written, the two chat tests are done, and Runtime needs has its date, attach this file in your lab chat and send the check prompt. The AI reads the tests' verdicts in the chat and quotes your document. You have the final say.

## The checks

Task 5 carries checks 14 to 17 of the lab's 20.

14. **Sam's chat message went to Dave, and the chat is a channel, nothing more.** A colleague asks for a vendor's bank change in the team chat app, and presses for speed. Neither changes the rule: a request to change payment details goes to Dave, however it arrives. Channels names the team chat app, and Triggers may say that a chat message starts work. Authority must not grow: a worker that may only draft by email may still only draft in the team chat. Missed: a worker that makes the change because a colleague asked, or a new Authority line added for the chat.
15. **Tom's amount change was refused without evidence, and routed.** The lab never discusses this message: it is the test of your contract alone. A colleague's say-so is not a document, and the worker cannot see the invoice from the chat. Passed: no register change, and the request goes to a person, so someone with the document decides. Naming the invoice helps that person, but is not required. Delaying until the invoice is checked passes too. The verdict must also have quoted a line of your contract: a safe answer under "no line decides it" is the AI's caution, not your contract. Add the line it was missing, and test again. Missed: the register updated, or a promise to update it, on the message's word.
16. **Runtime needs comes from what you ran, with the date, and says how the worker keeps going.** It names the app you used; the model and setting it showed, or "the app's default"; the real date you ran the tests, not the story's; and what it passed, the five message tests. It says how the standing role gets its continuity, one of Concept 2.4's two ways: an always-on agent that keeps its state, or scheduled runs that start fresh and rebuild from the records, with a reason. 2.4 gives Brightline's AP Worker the second, from the register and the policy, and the first passes with a reason. It ends with one line for the other AI vendor, a model chosen by job and marked "predicted, not run", and nothing outside Runtime needs changes for it. Missed: no date, no continuity, a setting with no app, or a vendor change that touched another field.
17. **The five parts sit in the fields Concept 2.4 gives them, and no AI vendor or model is named outside Runtime needs.** The connector that reads the inbox: Tools. Code execution for totals: Tools. The invoice check saved as a skill: Procedures (skills). The Monday schedule: Runtime needs, because a schedule is what runs the worker; Triggers may also say Monday morning, which is when. The project: Runtime needs, because the project is where it runs; the AP policy the project holds goes under Knowledge sources. Missed: a part in any other field, such as the schedule under Triggers alone, the project under Knowledge sources, the policy under Runtime needs, or a skill under Authority; or Claude, ChatGPT or a model named under Tools. A Runtime needs line that mentions scheduled runs in passing does not place part 4: the part itself must be named there. One move fixes it: name the part and the field, and send the check prompt again.

One afternoon's tests prove a provisional lab setting, not a production choice. Writing it as that passes.

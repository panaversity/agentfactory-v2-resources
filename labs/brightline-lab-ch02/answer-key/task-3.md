# Lab 2 answer key: Task 3. What it works with

The answers for Task 3 of Lab 2, in Chapter 2 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When the third group, What it works with, is written from your own answers, attach this file in your lab chat and send the check prompt. ## The checks

Task 3 carries checks 6 to 8 of the lab's 21.

6. **Knowledge sources names the AP policy and its version, or makes the version an open question.** The worker answers from what is officially true, and a policy without a version is two policies: the story has a March copy and a newer one. Passed: the AP policy with a version and date, or the policy with "which version?" under Open questions. Missed: "the policy" alone, or the March copy named as the source with no question.
7. **Memory says what the worker may remember, and what it must not.** Passed: both halves, in your words, such as "may remember which vendors send scanned PDFs; must not remember bank details". Missed: one half only, or "remembers everything it sees". Memory is experience, not the record: a remembered rule is not policy.
8. **Procedures and Tools name the steps, the systems and a choice for each of Dave's three needs, with why, and no AI vendor or model.** Passed: steps such as checking an invoice total, and the invoice check saved as a skill if you chose to save it; systems from "Where things are kept", such as the AP inbox, the shared AP folder with its register spreadsheet, and the accounting system; and Dave's three needs under Tools, each named by what it does, with why the one people often pick instead falls short (Concept 2.4's table). A connector reads the AP inbox, because a pasted or uploaded copy is out of date as soon as a new email arrives. Code execution adds up totals, because the model's own arithmetic can get a long sum wrong. Research checks a vendor Brightline has never paid across many sources, and cites them, because a quick web search suits one quick fact. The reasons may be in your words. Missed: a need left unanswered, answered with the choice that falls short, such as pasting emails in or a quick web search, or answered with no reason; a tool line that decides, such as "research approves new vendors", because a tool says what the worker can reach, and Authority, in Task 4, says what it may do; a feature named by its AI vendor, such as "Claude's Gmail connector"; or Claude, ChatGPT or a model name anywhere in this group. Those belong in Runtime needs, which Task 5 fills.

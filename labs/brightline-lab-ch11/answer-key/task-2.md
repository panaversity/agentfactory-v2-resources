# Lab 11 answer key: Task 2. Stop and hold

The answers for Task 2 of Lab 11, in Chapter 11 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When every item of the week has your answer, attach this file in your lab chat, and send the check prompt. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed, quoting your documents' lines and the replies in the chat. You have the final say. If a check passed only after you changed a line, you still get it: fixing the line is the skill this lab teaches.

## The checks

Task 2 carries checks 3 to 6 of the lab's 47.

3. **Both jobs paused, with a person doing each by hand.** The Thursday pre-run review and the invoice email check are each paused until the cause is known. A named person does each job's work by hand meanwhile, such as Maria checking each invoice email against the register, and Maria or Jordan preparing Thursday's note. **The hard rule.** Containment that changes a brief or switches the model fails this check: that is a fix, made before the cause is known.
    - Passed: both jobs paused, each with a named person, and the hard rule holds.
    - Missed: a job left running; a job paused with no one doing its work; or the hard rule broken.
4. **Drafted, or changed in a system.** Wednesday's three replies were only drafted: nothing went out. The other three changed something outside the chat: the saved note, which went to Dave; 7781-R's entry in the register; and the five replies Maria sent. Each change is checked where it happened: the notes folder or Dave's inbox, the register, and the sent folder, never the task record.
    - Passed: the drafts only drafted; the note, the entry and the sent replies changed a system; and each with where it is checked, the sent folder for the replies.
    - Missed: the drafts counted as sent; the note or the sent replies counted as drafts nobody acted on; or a change checked only in the record.
5. **7781-R held.** 7781-R is held out of the run of Friday, January 22, 2027, and checked against 7781 in the register. **The hard rule.** Deleting a record during containment, such as 7781-R's row, fails this check: the record is evidence.
    - Passed: 7781-R held from the January 22 run, with 7781 checked in the register, and the hard rule holds.
    - Missed: 7781-R left in the run; held with no check of 7781; or the hard rule broken.
6. **What went out is put right.** Tuesday's invoice emails are checked by hand for copies, because the check said nothing about them. Dave gets the real list of invoices due, from the live register, before the next run. Each of the five vendors gets a corrected reply with its own notice, which Maria sends.
    - Passed: all three.
    - Missed: Tuesday's emails left unchecked; Dave's note left as it was; or the five vendors left without a correction.

## Why

Lesson 11.1's order is stop, inspect, contain, correct, verify, resume. Containment comes before the cause, so it holds and checks, and fixes nothing yet. A brief changed now is a guess, and a deleted row is evidence gone.

"The record tells you what the worker reports it did. Only the connected systems tell you what actually changed." A draft in the drafts folder changed nothing. A saved note, a register row and a sent email each changed a system, so each is checked in that system. Record D says "Maria sent them"; the sent folder shows it.

A failure is every output since the fault began (Lesson 11.5). So Tuesday's silent emails, Dave's empty note and the five vendors' replies each need putting right, not only the next run.

## Never a deduction

- The order of your answers, or "hold" for "check" where you also say what you check.
- Jordan named instead of Maria, or both.
- Tuesday's emails described as "nothing changed", with the inbox as where to check.
- An extra step, such as asking IT to reconnect the drive, or telling Dave about 7781-R.
- A remark about the lab's dates.

# 20.15 How do the pieces fit together?

**In everyday life.** A shop has goods, staff who know how to sell them, a shop window, and a door with a lock. Each answers a different question, and the shop needs all four.

**What you will learn.** How the chapter's extensions fit one connector, and the mistakes builders make most. You write the four layers down in Lab 20, Task 12.

**Why it matters.** Each extension answers a different question. Mixing them up leads to the mistakes below, such as treating a screen as a permission.

### What does each layer answer?

| Question | Answer | Brightline example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What can the AI do? | Tools, plain MCP | `list_open_invoices`, `find_vendor` |
| How should the AI do the job well? | Skills over MCP | `invoice-triage` |
| What does the person see and touch? | MCP Apps, with A2UI if the AI shapes the screen | The open-invoices screen |
| Who may use it? | Sign-in, with Enterprise-Managed Authorization and Client Credentials | Luis, Cedar Ridge's employees, the report job |

### Who is acting, in each case?

| What happens | Who is acting | Who decided or approved | Lesson |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A person presses a button on the screen | The person signed in, for the company in their token | The connector checks it like any call | 20.4 |
| The model follows a skill the connector served | The person signed in. The skill only guides the model | The person, when their AI app asks them to approve those exact files. Whether it asks is the AI app's choice | 20.12 |
| An employee connects through their company | The employee | The company's policy, at its identity provider, decided they may connect | 20.13 |

### What are the common mistakes?

Four rows come from going further: three from 20.10, 20.13 and 20.14, and one from the last section of this page. Each row names where it is taught, so on the core path you can read the row and leave the lesson for later.

| Mistake | Why it hurts | Better |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A screen with no text answer | AI apps without screens show nothing useful | Every tool returns useful text (20.9) |
| A screen for every answer | The chat fills with panels | Show a screen when the person will look or act (20.2) |
| Treating a hidden tool or button as protected | Anyone with a token can call any tool | Check every call in the connector (20.5) |
| Depending on streamed input | Some AI apps never send it | Work from the complete input (20.3) |
| Using `window.openai` extras everywhere | The screen works only in ChatGPT | Build to the standard, and check before using an extra (20.9) |
| Letting the AI write the page | Layout, quality and safety vary | Approved parts, such as A2UI (20.10) |
| Generic skill names | Skills from different servers collide | Distinct names (20.12) |
| Changing skill files often | Every change means approving again | Batch and version changes (20.12) |
| Linking accounts by email alone | A reused address can open someone else's records | Issuer and subject (20.13) |
| A job that borrows a person's access | It acts as that person | Its own identity, with the least scope (20.14) |
| A checkout, or a check of who has paid, in the page | Anyone who uses the page can read it, and it cannot stop a call | Check the plan on the connector, on every call, and link to your plans page (below, and 17.9) |
| Testing only that the screen appears | A screen can appear and still lose the person's work | Test the whole path: sign in, act, reopen, retry |
| Testing in one AI app | Support differs feature by feature | Test every AI app and plan you promise, phones included |

### Where does the check of who has paid go?

*Going further. Read it if you plan to sell a connector with screens.*

A screen makes a product easier to sell. It must not become a way around paying. A gym's new app shows your classes, but your card is still checked at the door.

So the check stays where 17.9 put it: on the connector, on every call, after the token check. A screen's calls take the same path as the model's, so they get the same check (20.4). When a firm whose plan has ended opens the screen, the page asks for the first page of invoices, the plan check fails, and the tool returns its usual short error with a link to your plans page. The page shows that message and link. It shows no checkout.

Nothing in the page decides who has paid, because anyone who uses the page can read it. Payment stays on your own website. As of November 26, 2026, Claude does not support purchases through a connector's screens. ChatGPT's rules on selling inside an app are in How it works on Claude and on OpenAI.

**The operating rule:** let tools and sign-in decide what is allowed, let skills and screens make the work easier, keep the check of who has paid on the connector, and test each layer in every AI app you sell into.

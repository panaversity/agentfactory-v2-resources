# Lab 3 answer key: Task 1. What one line leaves open

The answers for Task 1 of Lab 3, in Chapter 3 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** Use it when the GUIDE says "Task 1 is done". Attach this file in your lab chat, type `Check` and send it. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed. It quotes your notebook, or your words in the chat. If you think a mark is wrong, the final decision is yours, not the AI's. Missed a check? Read "Why", below, and tell the GUIDE the change, in a few words. Then send the line the GUIDE shows: `Check again`. If a check passed only after you changed an answer, it still counts as Passed. Seeing what one line leaves open is the skill this task teaches.

## The checks

Task 1 carries checks 1 and 2 of the lab's 24.

1. **You asked for the plan, and questioned it with "what if" questions of two kinds or more** (Lesson 3.2, "How to do it, step by step", Steps 1 and 2). You sent `Show the plan`. The WORKER planned from Nadia's line and the folder's list of files, and nothing else. Then you asked "what if" questions, one at a time. Each question started with the number of its kind, from 1 to 5.
   - Passed: the plan is in the chat, and so are two or more "what if" questions, with at least two different numbers.
   - Missed: no plan; fewer than two questions; or every question with the same number.
2. **"What Nadia's line left open" names three gaps or more** (Lesson 3.2, "Why you cannot question your own setup"). A gap is something the worker had to decide or guess, because Nadia's line does not say it. Each choice the WORKER marked `nobody said it, so I guessed` is a gap, even when the guess was right. A question the worker asked you, instead of guessing, is a gap too. Gaps in this job include: today's date; how much space, and by when; which files may go; what to keep, such as Clients/Lakeview; whether it may delete, or only list; when to stop and ask; and what proof comes back.
   - Passed: three or more gaps, one per line, under "What Nadia's line left open".
   - Missed: fewer than three gaps. A line that names nothing the worker had to decide or guess, such as "the plan is good", is not a gap.

## Why

Nadia knows what she means. She knows today's date, how much space she needs, and by when. She knows which files she keeps, and that she deletes files herself. Her line says none of this. When you read your own request, you read what you meant, not what you wrote (Lesson 3.2). The worker has only the page. So it guesses, or it asks. A right guess is still a guess. On another day, or with another worker, it can come out another way.

In this lab, the WORKER shows where each choice came from. A real worker does not. Every gap you leave is a guess it will make alone (Lesson 3.2, "Two rules"). A plan costs about a minute. It shows the guesses before any file is touched. Each gap you named is a line your setup needs. You write those lines in Task 2.

## Never a deduction

These things never make you miss a check:

- What the plan says, or how good it is. Task 1 grades your questions and your gaps, not the plan.
- Which number you gave each question. The check only asks that two of your questions have different numbers.
- A question with no number. It does not add a kind, and it takes nothing away.
- More than three questions.
- A gap the worker guessed right. A right guess is still a guess.
- A gap you found in Nadia's box, not in the worker's answers. It counts, if her line does not say it.
- Your words, your spelling, and the order of your gaps.

Before you hand over a one-line request, ask for a plan, and ask two "what if" questions. Each guess you find is a line your request is missing.

# Lab 3 answer key: Task 9. Your own task, alone

The answers for Task 9 of Lab 3, in Chapter 3 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** Use it in the fresh chat, not in the lab chat. Attach it only when the fresh chat has given its plan and answered your two "what if" questions, and you have judged each answer. Then the key cannot change the answers. Attach this file in the fresh chat, and send the check prompt from the page. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed. It quotes "My own task" and your messages in the fresh chat. Your task is your own, so no key can hold its answers: each check says what a good setup has. If you think a mark is wrong, the final decision is yours, not the AI's. If a check passed only after you changed something, it still counts as Passed. Setting up your own work is the skill this task teaches.

**After a miss.** Checks 22 and 23 grade your file. Go back to your lab chat, and tell the GUIDE the change, such as `Scope: <your new line>`. Download "My own task" again, or copy all its text again. If your fresh chat is still open, send `Read the newest "My own task", and check it again against the key.` there, with the new file attached, or with its text pasted below the line. If it is gone, open a new fresh chat with memory off, and do Task 9's steps 6 to 10 on the page again, with the new file or its text. Check 24 grades what you did in the fresh chat. Do the missing piece there, such as judging an answer. Then send the check prompt again.

## The checks

Task 9 carries checks 22 to 24 of the lab's 24.

22. **Your setup has all four parts, and each part does its job** (Lesson 3.2, the four parts and "The review contract"). The four parts are Intent, Scope, Authority and Review contract. The review contract answers five questions: 1. What must be checked? 2. What evidence comes back? 3. What counts as success? 4. What makes the worker stop and ask? 5. What must never happen automatically? "My own task" holds:
    - **Your task:** one piece of your own work, with an end. It may repeat, such as a weekly report. It is not a whole role, such as "be my assistant", and not several tasks. It is not one of this lab's three jobs: Nadia's shared folder, Leila's café reviews or Sana's Monday sales report.
    - **A line under each of the four headings**, and an answer to each of the five questions. "None", or a blank, is not an answer.
    - **Why it matters:** the reason for the work.
    - **Something out:** at least one thing that is outside the job.
    - **A limit:** the setup says what the worker may do on its own, and it is not everything. "It may draft the replies" counts. "Anything it needs" or "full access" does not.
    - **A stop rule:** the answer to question 4. It names a case the worker can see in its work. For example: a missing file, two sources that disagree, or an amount over a set limit. The worker stops and asks you, unless the setup names someone else. Does your setup say the work runs when nobody is there to answer? Then the stop rule also says what the worker does instead of asking, such as a flag in what it brings back.
    - **A "never automatically" line:** the answer to question 5. It names at least one thing that must never happen on its own.
    - Passed: all seven. Your words, and which heading a line sits under, are not graded.
    - Missed: any one left out; a heading with nothing under it; a stop rule with nothing the worker can see, such as "if there is a problem" or "when needed"; or a stop rule that waits for a person when your setup says nobody will be there. "If you are not sure" or "if anything is unclear" passes only when it says about what, such as "if you are not sure which price is right".

23. **What you would question first fits your task's main risk** (Lesson 3.2, "How to choose"). At the end of "My own task", under "Not for the worker", your answer names the task's main risk, what you would question first at work, and why. "How to choose" says:
    - "If the risk is a wrong **action**, a plan is usually enough."
    - "If the risk is wrong **quality** or judgment, ask for a sample."
    - "If the worker will run **alone or repeatedly**, or a mistake would be expensive, do a dry run."
    - Passed: a risk, a check and a why, and the check is the one "How to choose" gives for your risk, as you describe it. Two checks pass when one of them fits. For example: a plan first, because it is cheap, then a dry run, because the work runs every week. When your risk is of two kinds, a check that fits either kind passes.
    - Missed: no risk, no check or no why; or a check that does not fit the risk, such as only a plan for a risk in the quality of the writing, or only a sample for work that runs alone every week.

    What you question in the fresh chat is not graded. In this lab, you question a plan there, whatever you chose, because a sample or a dry run needs real data.

24. **Two "what if" questions, each answer judged, and each gap named with its part** (Lesson 3.2, "How to do it, step by step", Steps 2 to 4). In the fresh chat, after the plan:
    - **Two "what if" questions**, each starting with a kind's number or name: 1 the unusual case, 2 the stop, 3 the boundary, 4 the threshold, 5 the evidence. Whether the kind fits the question is not graded.
    - **One of them about the mistake that cost you most** in this kind of work. The AI cannot know your work, so your words must say which question it is, in the question or in a message after it.
    - **Each answer judged:** clear, vague or wrong. An answer is clear only when your lines decide it. If its "Where each choice came from" has "nobody said it, so I guessed" in it, it is a gap, even when it sounds right.
    - **Each gap named, with its part:** for each answer you called vague or wrong, the gap in a few words, and the part of your setup where its line belongs. Which part you name is not graded.
    - Passed: all four. A gap you have not fixed yet still counts: naming it is the skill.
    - Missed: fewer than two questions; a question with no kind; no words that say which question came from the mistake that cost you most; an answer not judged; an answer called clear when it has "nobody said it, so I guessed" in it; or a gap with no part.

## Why

**The fresh chat had only your file.** It could not see the lab, your notebook or your lab chat. A real worker is the same: it has only the page (Lesson 3.2). So each choice it made came from one of your lines, or it was a guess.

**The risk decides what you question first.** A plan shows what the worker will do. A sample shows its judgment. A dry run shows how it copes with real, messy data (Lesson 3.2). Start with the cheapest check that can show your risk. In this lab, you question a plan in the fresh chat, because real data stays out of the lab. At work, use the check that fits your risk.

**Your best questions come from your own work.** The mistake that cost you most is a case the worker cannot know about (Lesson 3.2). A right guess is still a guess. Each guess is a line your setup lacks, so write it in the setup, not in the chat. Then every run has it. Look at the plan's "Where each choice came from" too: each "nobody said it, so I guessed" there is a line your setup lacks. In all three jobs, one of those lines was the date.

**The jobs were only places to practice.** The rhythm is the same in your own work. You own both ends, and the worker owns the middle (Lesson 3.1, Figure 3.1).

## Never a deduction

These things never make you miss a check:

- Which task you chose, and how big or small it is.
- How good the worker's plan or answers were. Task 9 grades your setup and your judgments, not the worker.
- Where a fact sits in your file, and your exact words.
- Calling an answer vague when it was wrong, or wrong when it was vague. Both are gaps.
- Gaps you named and have not fixed yet.
- More gaps that you named from the plan, or a third "what if" question, as long as each answer is judged.
- That you questioned a plan in the fresh chat, whatever you would question first at work.
- Invented names, numbers or dates. This key does not check if something is real, because the AI cannot tell. Before you share your file, check it yourself: no real names, numbers or customer data.
- Which AI you used, and which plan.

## Take it to work

Before you hand over a task, give a fresh chat only your setup, and ask about the mistake that cost you most. Each guess in its answer is a line your setup needs.

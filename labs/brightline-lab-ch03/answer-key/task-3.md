## Why

"Completed" means the worker stopped without crashing. It says nothing about whether the numbers are right (Lesson 3.4). This run says Completed, and its total says 3.2 GB, more than the 3 GB Nadia needs. But two files on the list do not belong, so the real total is 1.6 GB.

The order is what finds this. The flag comes first: there, the worker tells you where it was unsure. The list comes next: each file on it waits for approval. Then the evidence, the riskiest file first. The total comes last, because a correction found earlier may change it (Lesson 3.4). Here, two corrections change it from 3.2 GB to 1.6 GB.

Each problem is fixed where it came from. A wrong row is fixed in place. A list that falls short goes back to the worker. A problem that came from the setup is fixed in the setup. Taking Team day copy.zip off fixes this list only. The next run will still trust a copy's name, unless your setup says how to tell a real copy. So you also keep the case as a test. After each change to the setup, the worker must still keep that file off the list.

Approval comes last. A named person says yes before anything is deleted. Here, that is Nadia. Never approve because the status says "Completed".

## Never a deduction

These things never make you miss a check:

- Where your answers sit in the notebook, and your words.
- The list and the evidence in either order, when the flag is first and the total last.
- Which file you checked first, when your reason names its risk.
- Asking Nadia about the Clients/Lakeview files, as well as keeping them off.
- A sum that is a little off, when your total is under 3 GB and says "no".
- More setup lines than this key asks for, such as one that says what "old" means.
- More than one case kept as a test.
- A line from Task 2 that already tells a real copy. You do not need it twice.
- What the run got wrong. Task 3 grades your review, not the run.

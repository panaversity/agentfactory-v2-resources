## The autonomy ladder (Lesson 7.2)

Show this table when a question below says "7.2's ladder":

| Rung | What the worker does | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observe | Reads and reports | Nothing |
| Recommend | Proposes a decision a person makes | Nothing yet |
| Draft | Prepares the thing itself, ready for a person to release | Nothing until a person releases it |
| Execute | Takes the action | Something outside the conversation |

"Execute comes in two forms. The worker acts alone, or it acts after a person approves each action. Approval each time is still execute, because once you say yes, the worker sends. At draft, a person sends."

Anthropic, as verified 7 October 2026:

- *The conversation setting.* Manual asks you before each action, except for tools you set to Always allow. Auto keeps working, with automated safety checks before each action. Cowork, where Claude carries out longer tasks for you, and Claude in Chrome add a third mode, Skip all approvals, in which Claude asks nothing and no safety check runs before it acts.
- *Per-tool control (7.4).* In the settings of each connector, a link from Claude to another app, you set each tool or group of tools to Always allow, Needs approval or Blocked. On Team and Enterprise plans, organization owners can set the same choices for the whole organization. Read tools are grouped apart from write and delete tools, and members cannot change the owners' choices. On those plans, Cowork asks before connector tools that can write, in every task, unless an organization owner allows Always allow for them.
- *Consequential actions (7.2).* Cowork asks before permanently deleting files. Anthropic says Claude in Chrome is built not to make purchases, create accounts, delete permanently or follow instructions found in emails or web pages. Anthropic also says it is built to ask before changing permissions or entering sensitive information. These statements describe how it is meant to behave. They are not a guarantee.
- *Tool order (7.5).* Anthropic's computer-use page puts connectors first, as the fastest and most reliable path, then the browser, then the screen. Computer use, on the Pro and Max plans, asks before each app and blocks investment and cryptocurrency apps by default. It has no sandbox, or closed-off space, between Claude and your apps.
- *Prompt injection (7.6).* Anthropic's Cowork safety page says an attack needs two things at once. Claude reads information from outside your trusted boundary, and Claude can take actions that could harm you. Its research says no browser agent is immune, or fully protected.
- *Runs no one watches.* You can choose an approval mode when you set up a Cowork scheduled task by hand. But the help page does not say what happens when an approval is needed and nobody is there. Anthropic advises against scheduling tasks that send messages or are hard to undo. Claude Code routines, a tool for developers, run without permission prompts and act as you.
- *Admin policy (7.4).* Owners choose which connectors members may use, and on Enterprise, custom roles can make tool permissions stricter, never looser.
- *Standing worker, verified 23 October 2026.* Claude Duet, in research preview, keeps working after you close its app. Before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document or deletes anything, it shows exactly what it will do. Then it waits for your approval. If you cannot approve at once, it finishes the rest of the task and brings the approval back to you later. Some actions are refused even with approval, such as trading securities or moving money between accounts.

OpenAI, as verified 8 October 2026:

- *The conversation setting.* In the desktop app, a permissions control sets local actions, the actions on your own computer. Its choices are Ask for approval, Approve for me or Full access. Approve for me sends a request to an automatic review instead of to you. OpenAI's prompting guide also advises adding a rule to your prompt. The rule requires your approval before ChatGPT sends, publishes or changes information other people rely on. That is advice about prompts, not a setting.
- *Per-tool control (7.4).* Each app, OpenAI's name for a connector, can have one of four levels: Always ask, Allow read actions, Allow low-risk actions or Allow all actions. Allow read actions reads without asking and asks before any change. At Allow low-risk actions, the product decides which actions count as low risk.
- *Consequential actions (7.2).* ChatGPT is designed to ask you before actions that are hard to undo or that create a real-world commitment, such as a payment. When it works in its cloud browser, a browser on a separate computer in the cloud, it asks in the chat. These statements describe how it is meant to behave. They are not a guarantee. OpenAI lists sending messages, changing records, changing access, payments and sharing sensitive data as higher-risk actions.
- *Tool order (7.5).* OpenAI advises using a connected app instead of the cloud browser when the app can do the task directly. ChatGPT may also make that choice on its own. In Atlas, OpenAI's web browser, agent mode pauses on sensitive sites such as banks.
- *Prompt injection (7.6).* OpenAI says an attack needs a source, a way to influence the system, and a sink, a capability that becomes dangerous in the wrong context. It says dangerous actions, or sending sensitive data, should not happen silently or without appropriate safeguards. Lockdown Mode limits outgoing requests. OpenAI says its app safeguards do not remove prompt-injection risk.
- *Runs no one watches.* A scheduled task may pause when one of its actions needs approval.
- *Admin policy (7.4).* Admins choose which read or write actions each app may use, and how new actions are treated. In workspace agents, write actions are set to Always ask by default.
- *Standing worker, verified 23 October 2026.* A dot, OpenAI's always-on agent, starts with built-in rules for when to act alone and when to ask. Its Custom Rules let you allow an action, require approval for it or block it. A check called auto-review looks at actions that could affect your accounts or share information. It checks them against your instructions, your Custom Rules and OpenAI's safety requirements. Custom Rules cannot turn off the core safety requirements. The most sensitive actions, such as changing a password or moving money, need you to take over and finish them yourself. Approving one message gives the dot no ongoing permission. An approval given in advance covers only what you authorized.

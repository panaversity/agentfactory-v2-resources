# Lab 6 answer key: Task 1. The checks, before you look

The answers for Task 1 of Lab 6, in Chapter 6 of *The AI Agent Factory*, Second Edition. Every name, number and company in this lab is invented.

**How this key is used.** When your Review Contract has all four parts and the AI has said "Task 1 is done", attach this file in your lab chat, and send the check prompt. The AI marks each check Passed or Missed, quoting your Review Contract and your answers in the chat. This key grades your contract against Maria's brief and Lesson 6.1, not against a list of good checks. You have the final say. If a check passed only after you changed your contract, you still get it: setting the checks is the skill this task teaches.

## The checks

Task 1 carries checks 1 to 4 of the lab's 30.

1. **The four parts, written before the package was shown.** Your Review Contract has at least one line under each of its four parts: what must be checked, what evidence comes back, what counts as success, and what stops the worker. You wrote it in Task 1, and no line from inside the package's four files appeared in the chat before it. The package's file names, and what each file is, do not count as lines from inside it.
   - Passed: a line under each of the four parts, and nothing from inside the package shown in the chat before the contract was written.
   - Missed: a part with no line; or a line from inside the package shown in the chat before the contract was written.
   - What is absent cannot be quoted. For the second half, read every message up to the one that shows the whole contract, and quote that message's first line when none of them holds a line from inside package/.
2. **Each rule the money and the decisions rest on has a check, with its yardstick.** A yardstick is the file the work is checked against. Your contract has a lettered line that checks each of these five rules of Maria's brief, against the file named here:
   - every open invoice gets one action, with a reason and a policy section: against the open invoices (open-invoices-2026-10-26.csv) or the policy (ap-policy-v3-excerpt.md)
   - the run covers invoices due on or before Friday, November 6, 2026, on the terms in the vendor record: the terms or the due dates against the vendor records (vendor-records.csv)
   - an approval counts only if it is in the approvals log: against the approvals log (approvals-log.csv)
   - a case version 3 does not cover is listed for Dave: against the policy (ap-policy-v3-excerpt.md)
   - every row reflects a decision Maria made: against her decisions, her message in the task record
   - Passed: all five, each against the file named here. One check may cover more than one rule. A file may be named in your own words, such as "the vendor record" or "Maria's message". A line checked against several files passes when the file named here is among them.
   - Missed: any of the five with no check, with no file named, or against another file than the one named here, such as the terms checked against the open invoices, whose terms column is the invoice's own.
3. **Full contract, every check, and Dave signs.** The depth at the top of your contract is the first row of Lesson 6.1's table: "Full contract, every check, a named human signs". The package moves money, because the accounting system pays from the CSV. The person named to sign is Dave, who approves the run.
   - Passed: the first row, and Dave named as the person who signs.
   - Missed: another row, such as the second because the memo feeds Dave's decision; or no one named, or someone other than Dave named as the person who signs.
4. **Maria's brief carries the evidence and the stops, and a reason, a section and a source on every row.** Under "Share with the worker", your contract marks for the brief every line under "What evidence comes back" and every line under "What stops the worker" (Lesson 6.1). The lines marked for the brief, one line or several together, ask for a reason, a policy section and a source on every row of the proposal (Lesson 6.8).
   - Passed: every evidence line and every stop line marked for the brief, and the marked lines, together, asking for a reason, a policy section and a source on every row. "The file and row it came from" counts as a source.
   - Missed: an evidence line or a stop line not marked for the brief; or the marked lines, together, do not ask for all three on every row.

## Why

Once you have seen a result, you compare it only with itself (Lesson 6.1). Last week Dave had no list, so the memo set its own standard. This week you wrote the checks before one line of the package was shown. Each check names its yardstick, because a check against the work itself finds only what the work already says.

Maria's brief already holds the rules the run rests on: one action per invoice with a reason and a section, the run's window, terms from the vendor record, approvals only from the log, cases the policy does not cover listed for Dave, and her own decisions on every row. A check for each, against the file that decides it, is what lets the review find what matters.

The depth follows what is at risk. The CSV moves money, so every check runs, and a named human, Dave, signs. Lesson 6.1's own example puts the memo Dave uses in the second row. This package also holds the CSV the accounting system pays from, so it is the first row. When the answer is unclear, choose the deeper level. The evidence and the stops go into the brief, because the worker acts on them. A reason, a policy section and a source on every row is the line Lesson 6.8 says every contract needs, because neither AI vendor promises a citation to a row in your own file.

## Never a deduction

- A check of your own that this key does not name.
- One check that covers several rules, or one rule checked twice.
- Your own words for a check, a file or a depth.
- Your own name beside Dave's at the top of the contract.
- A line of Maria's brief you chose not to check, such as the note's three lines, when the five rules above have their checks.
- A success test or a stop that this key does not name.

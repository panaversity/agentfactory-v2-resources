## The check

Task 12 carries check 31 of the lab's 31.

31. **The four source checks, and Dave's answer.** In your lab chat:
    - The source behind "about 60 percent" is the 2018 survey, source 4. Sources 1 to 3 give no survey of their own.
    - Sources 1 to 3 repeat one sentence, word for word, so they count as one source.
    - Source 4 is from 2018: too old to plan next year's cash on.
    - Source 5, from April 2026, disagrees. It found that 22 percent agreed to longer terms when asked, and that 41 percent raised prices for buyers that asked. The report never says so.
    - The main claim does not hold up, or holds only partly. Dave should not plan on net 45, and should ask supplier by supplier.
    - Passed: all four checks, and Dave's answer.
    - Missed: the main claim accepted; sources 1 to 3 counted as three; source 4 not named as too old; or source 5 not named.

## Why

A research report reads as one sure answer, but its sources are mixed. Some are official, some are old, and some copy each other (Lesson 6.5). So check the claim a decision rests on, not every footnote. Here that claim is "about 60 percent", because Dave will plan next year's cash on it.

Open the source behind it. Three of the four pages the report cites repeat one sentence, so they are one source, and none of them ran a survey. The survey they lean on is from 2018. Read closely, it also says less than the claim: 61 percent "would consider" longer terms, which is not the same as agreeing to net 45.

Prefer the newer and more official source. The 2026 survey asked 430 suppliers, and found that 22 percent agreed. It also found that asking can cost something, which the report's last line denies. Where good sources disagree, a report should say so. This one picks a side without saying why, so the claim is unsupported, and Dave decides supplier by supplier.

## Never a deduction

- The words of Dave's next step, when it does not plan on net 45.
- "Partly" for the main claim, when your line says what is left of it.
- Naming source 1, from 2019, as too old as well.
- Saying yes or no to whether source 4 says what the claim says, when your line names source 4 as the survey behind the figure.

## Take it to work

Before you plan on research, check the source behind the claim your plan rests on.

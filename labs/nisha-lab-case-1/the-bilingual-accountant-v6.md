## Preface

### The problem

There are two main sets of accounting rules in the world. More than 140 jurisdictions (countries and regions) require IFRS Accounting Standards (IFRS), says the [IFRS Foundation](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/around-the-world/roadmap-development-tool/jurisdictional-roadmapdevelopmenttool-2025.pdf) (March 2025). Each place decides which companies must use them. US Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (US GAAP) are used mainly in the United States.

US companies listed in the US must use US GAAP ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/concept/2025/33-11376.pdf)). Some foreign companies listed in the US can use IFRS ([SEC rule](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2007/12/acceptance-foreign-private-issuers-financial-statements-prepared-accordance-international-financial)). Chapter 1 explains the rules.

I am from Pakistan, and I trained in IFRS. I qualified as a chartered accountant with a gold medal from the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Pakistan (ICAP). I want to work with US companies and the Big 4 firms (Deloitte, EY, KPMG and PwC), so I must know US GAAP well. I am writing this book while I make that move myself. My aim is to help other accountants make the same move with confidence.

I am not the only one. Many skilled accountants know one framework well but need the other for their work. A list of differences is not enough for them. They need help to make the move.

People often say that IFRS is based on principles and US GAAP is based on rules. This is only partly true. Chapter 2 explains why.

### Who this book is for

This book is mainly for accountants trained in IFRS who want to work with US GAAP. For example, chartered accountants who want to join US companies or Big 4 firms. It is also for accountants trained in US GAAP who now need to work with IFRS. This direction gets less space. These readers will find a short box in each topic chapter, help with converting statements in Chapters 21 and 22, and career advice in Chapter 24.

This book compares full IFRS Accounting Standards with US GAAP for businesses. It does not cover IFRS for SMEs (small and medium-sized entities) or the accounting options for US private companies.

You should already know basic accounting. Many readers speak English as a second language, so I use short sentences and plain words.

### What makes this book different

- **It teaches the move.** You learn how to think in the new framework, find answers in its rules, and convert financial statements.
- **It explains why.** Where it helps, the book explains why a major difference exists, so it is easier to remember.
- **It uses the same example twice.** Each major topic shows one example under both frameworks, using journal entries, extracts from financial statements, or disclosure examples.
- **It helps your career.** It covers research, SEC reporting, interviews and the qualifications you may need.

### How to use this book

- **Part I** gives the background and the vocabulary. Read it first.
- **Part II** has one chapter for each topic. Read the chapters by topic. Two exceptions: read Chapter 12 before Chapter 13, and for goodwill read Chapter 18 before Chapter 10. Each topic chapter follows the same pattern: why it matters, where the frameworks agree, where they differ, the reason where it helps, a worked example, two short boxes ("If you know IFRS" and "If you know US GAAP"), common traps, and exercises.
- **Part III** shows how to convert financial statements and how to plan your career in either direction.
- **The appendices** are for quick checks.

Both frameworks keep changing. For example, [IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-18-presentation-and-disclosure-in-financial-statements/) for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. Always check the current text of a standard before you rely on it.

Nisha

## Table of contents

### How to read the short forms

Each chapter shows its main standards in brackets. The IFRS reference comes first, then a slash, then the US GAAP reference. For example, (IAS 2 / ASC 330) means IAS 2 for IFRS and ASC 330 for US GAAP. Appendix A gives the full list for every chapter. A chapter that is not about specific standards has no brackets. The references show guidance that is relevant to the chapter. They do not mean that the two standards have the same scope or the same rules.

**On the IFRS side**

- **IFRS:** an IFRS Accounting Standard, for example IFRS 15. The IASB issues them. The IASB is part of the IFRS Foundation.
- **IAS:** an International Accounting Standard, for example IAS 2. These are older IFRS standards. Many are still in force.
- **IFRIC:** an official interpretation of IFRS, for example IFRIC 23.
- **IFRS S1 and IFRS S2:** IFRS sustainability standards, issued by the International Sustainability Standards Board (ISSB). They are separate from IFRS Accounting Standards. Some places require them, and some companies use them voluntarily.

**On the US GAAP side**

- **US GAAP:** Generally Accepted Accounting Principles in the United States.
- **FASB:** the Financial Accounting Standards Board. It sets accounting standards for businesses and other non-government organizations in the US.
- **ASC:** the FASB Accounting Standards Codification, the official source of US GAAP for non-government organizations. Companies that report to the SEC must also follow SEC rules. ASC 330 means Topic 330, and 835-20 points to a subtopic.
- **ASU:** an Accounting Standards Update, for example ASU 2024-03. It explains how the ASC is changing, and when. The rules themselves are in the ASC.

**Other short forms (these are not accounting standards)**

- **SEC:** the US Securities and Exchange Commission, the regulator of US public companies.
- **Regulation S-X:** SEC rules on the form and content of financial statements.
- **PCAOB:** the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board. It oversees the audits of public companies and of SEC-registered brokers and dealers, and sets auditing standards for them.
- **Sarbanes-Oxley Act:** a US law on internal controls and audits of public companies.
- **SEC Financial Reporting Manual:** a guide written by SEC staff. It is not US GAAP.
- **ISAs:** International Standards on Auditing. They are audit standards, not accounting standards.

### Part I: Background on the two frameworks

**Chapter 1. The two frameworks and who uses them**

Why there are two sets of rules, who writes them, and which companies must use which.

**Chapter 2. Principles-based and rules-based: what the words really mean**

Where each framework asks you to use judgment, and where it gives fixed rules. It also explains the conceptual framework behind each one.

**Chapter 3. How to find answers: the standards and the vocabulary**

How to look up answers in IFRS and the ASC, and how the same word can mean different things. For example, "stock" means inventories in British English but shares in the US.

**Chapter 4. US financial reporting: the SEC, auditors and controls**

The SEC, the main report forms, the SEC rules for financial statements (Regulation S-X), auditing under the PCAOB, and internal controls under the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.

### Part II: The main differences, topic by topic

**Chapter 5. Presentation of financial statements and cash flows**

How the main financial statements are laid out, how the cash flow statement differs, and how to treat changes in accounting policies and errors. It covers IAS 1 and IFRS 18, which applies to years beginning on or after 1 January 2027 and can be used earlier. (IFRS 18, IAS 7, IAS 8 / ASC 220, ASC 230, ASC 250)

**Chapter 6. Revenue**

When to record revenue and how much. The two frameworks are close here, but not the same. (IFRS 15 / ASC 606)

**Chapter 7. Inventory**

How to value goods held for sale, which cost methods are allowed, and what happens when values fall. (IAS 2 / ASC 330)

**Chapter 8. Property, plant and equipment, and investment property**

How to measure, depreciate and revalue long-term assets, and how to treat borrowing costs and government grants. (IAS 16, IAS 40, IAS 23 / ASC 360, ASC 835-20)

**Chapter 9. Intangible assets and research and development**

When costs become assets, and how research and development costs are treated. Goodwill is covered in Chapter 18, where it arises, and in Chapter 10, on impairment. (IAS 38 / ASC 350, ASC 730)

**Chapter 10. Impairment, assets held for sale and discontinued operations**

How to test assets for a loss in value, and whether the loss can be reversed later. It also explains how to measure and present assets held for sale and discontinued operations. (IAS 36, IFRS 5 / ASC 360, ASC 350)

**Chapter 11. Leases**

How lessees (who use leased assets) and lessors (who provide them) record leases, and how this changes key ratios. (IFRS 16 / ASC 842)

**Chapter 12. Financial instruments I: classification, measurement and credit losses**

How to classify and measure financial assets, how to record expected credit losses, and what to disclose about financial instruments. (IFRS 9, IFRS 7 / ASC 320, ASC 321, ASC 326)

**Chapter 13. Financial instruments II: derivatives, hedging, debt and equity**

How to record derivatives and hedges, how to tell debt from equity (including a company's own shares), and how to measure fair value. (IFRS 9, IAS 32, IFRS 13 / ASC 815, ASC 480, ASC 820)

**Chapter 14. Provisions, contingencies and events after the reporting period**

When to record a liability that is uncertain, and how to treat events after the year end. (IAS 37, IAS 10 / ASC 450, ASC 855)

**Chapter 15. Income taxes**

How to record current and deferred tax, and how to treat uncertain tax positions. (IAS 12, IFRIC 23 / ASC 740)

**Chapter 16. Employee benefits and share-based payment**

How to record pensions and other benefits, and pay that is given in shares or options. (IAS 19, IFRS 2 / ASC 715, ASC 718)

**Chapter 17. Foreign currency and hyperinflation**

How to choose the functional currency (the main currency a company works in), translate foreign operations, and deal with very high inflation. (IAS 21, IAS 29 / ASC 830)

**Chapter 18. Business combinations and consolidation**

How to account for buying a business, including goodwill, and which companies belong in the consolidated financial statements. It also covers joint arrangements, the equity method, and IFRS 19, which lets eligible subsidiaries give fewer disclosures. (IFRS 3, IFRS 10, IFRS 11 / ASC 805, ASC 810, ASC 323)

**Chapter 19. Earnings per share, segments, interim reporting and related parties**

How to report earnings per share, business segments, interim periods and related parties. (IAS 33, IFRS 8, IAS 34 / ASC 260, ASC 280, ASC 270)

**Chapter 20. Rules for special industries**

A short introduction to selected rules for insurance, mining, oil and gas, and farming, and how they work with the general standards. Appendix A lists the references.

### Part III: Moving from one framework to the other

**Chapter 21. Converting financial statements between the frameworks**

A step-by-step method to convert statements from one framework to the other, in both directions, with a full case study.

**Chapter 22. When a company changes its framework**

How a company moves to a new framework and how to plan the project. It covers IFRS 1 for companies adopting IFRS for the first time, and the SEC rules for a foreign company that must switch to US GAAP. (IFRS 1 / SEC Financial Reporting Manual, Topic 6)

**Chapter 23. Career guide A: from IFRS to US GAAP**

The steps to work in a US company or a Big 4 US practice: qualifications, skills, interviews and the first 90 days. It includes SEC reporting and the Sarbanes-Oxley Act.

**Chapter 24. Career guide B: from US GAAP to IFRS**

The steps for US GAAP accountants who move to IFRS work, and the habits to change. It includes a short note on the ISAs.

**Chapter 25. What is changing**

What to learn now: IFRS 18 on presentation, ASU 2024-03 on more detailed expense disclosures by public companies that use US GAAP, and how sustainability reporting is developing. The SEC has paused its climate rules and has proposed to withdraw them. (IFRS 18, IFRS S1, IFRS S2 / ASU 2024-03)

### Appendices

- **A. Master differences and reference tables.** Short comparisons for each topic, then the full list of IFRS and US GAAP references for every chapter.
- **B. Terminology dictionary.** Plain-English meanings of important accounting terms, how IFRS and US GAAP use them, and British and American alternatives. For example, "inventories" and "inventory", and the different meanings of "stock".
- **C. Journal-entry quick reference.** For both frameworks.
- **D. Self-test and interview questions.** With answers.
- **E. Reading list.** Links to standard-setters and regulators.
- **F. Excel conversion toolkit.** Reconciliation templates.

- SEC: [Acceptance of IFRS financial statements from foreign private issuers without reconciliation to US GAAP (2007 final rule)](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2007/12/acceptance-foreign-private-issuers-financial-statements-prepared-accordance-international-financial).

Sections 6120.2 and 6120.4: a company that loses foreign private issuer status must follow the rules for domestic companies from the first day of its next fiscal year, and must recast (present again) its financial statements into US GAAP for all periods presented.

- FASB: [In Focus: Disaggregation of Income Statement Expenses (ASU 2024-03)](https://storage.fasb.org/FASB%20In%20Focus%20Disaggregation%20of%20Income%20Statement%20Expenses.pdf). Applies to public business entities.

- IFRS Foundation: [Introduction to the ISSB and IFRS Sustainability Disclosure Standards](https://www.ifrs.org/sustainability/knowledge-hub/introduction-to-issb-and-ifrs-sustainability-disclosure-standards/). The ISSB issued IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 in June 2023. The page says they are separate from IFRS Accounting Standards and are used through regulatory adoption and voluntary use.
- FASB: [ASU 2025-01, Clarifying the Effective Date](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU%202025-01.pdf). ASU 2024-03 applies to annual periods beginning after 15 December 2026 and interim periods within annual periods beginning after 15 December 2027. Early adoption is permitted.
